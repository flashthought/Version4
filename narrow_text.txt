Clinical Overview & Motivation
Incomplete Observability of the Hemodynamic State in Septic Shock
Septic shock is characterised by rapidly changing and heterogeneous cardiovascular dysfunction. Hypotension may result from intravascular volume depletion, profound vasodilation, impaired vascular tone, myocardial dysfunction, altered arterial properties, or combinations of these mechanisms. These changes can evolve during disease progression and in response to interventions such as fluids and vasopressors.
Arterial blood pressure provides an important but incomplete view of this underlying hemodynamic state. For example, an increase in cardiac output following fluid administration does not necessarily produce a corresponding increase in mean arterial pressure if systemic vascular resistance or arterial load changes simultaneously. Consequently, important hemodynamic quantities, including vascular resistance and arterial compliance, are not continuously observed at the bedside and must be inferred from available measurements and their responses over time.
This creates an incomplete-observability problem: the physiological state governing pressure and perfusion is only partially represented by the signals that can be measured continuously.
The proposed work therefore investigates whether computational methods can infer this latent cardiovascular state from routinely available physiological waveforms, while incorporating known cardiovascular relationships as physical constraints. Rather than treating septic shock prediction as a conventional classification problem, the study focuses on the underlying inverse problem: what aspects of the latent hemodynamic state can be recovered from observable physiological signals, and what additional observations are required to make that recovery identifiable?
The clinical motivation comes from septic shock and other forms of hemodynamic instability, but the methodological development will initially be performed using fully public datasets and controlled simulation. This allows the physiological inference problem to be investigated without depending on access to a large, annotated septic-shock cohort.

Refined Project Scope
Proposed Title
Physics-Informed Estimation of Latent Hemodynamic States from Multimodal Physiological Signals

Primary Research Question
Can routinely available physiological waveforms, including ECG, PPG and arterial blood pressure, support physics-informed estimation of latent hemodynamic parameters, particularly vascular resistance and arterial compliance, and how does parameter identifiability change as additional observations and intervention information are incorporated?

Secondary Research Question
Can the estimated latent hemodynamic state support short-horizon forecasting of arterial pressure dynamics?

Scope and Boundaries
The study will:
Focus on ECG, PPG and arterial blood pressure (ABP) as the primary observable signals.
Investigate latent hemodynamic parameters, particularly systemic vascular resistance and arterial compliance.
Examine arterial load and short-term blood-pressure dynamics as related state variables or downstream outcomes.
Use a reduced-order cardiovascular simulator as the ground-truth environment for controlled parameter-recovery and identifiability experiments.
Compare progressively richer observation regimes to determine how additional signals improve latent-state estimation.
Investigate the potential value of intervention information, such as fluid or vasopressor administration, where reliably available.
Treat septic shock as the primary clinical motivation and potential future application rather than requiring a large septic-shock cohort in the current methodological stage.

The study will not:
Depend on a large annotated septic-shock cohort.
Make clinical claims about septic-shock diagnosis or treatment.
Attempt to estimate every possible cardiovascular parameter.
Include credentialed or restricted databases during the current stage.
Treat model performance on public waveforms as evidence of clinical readiness.

Core Technical Framework
The proposed work treats the problem as a physics-informed inverse problem.

Observable physiological signals:
ECG + PPG + ABP
↓
Signal processing and feature extraction
↓
Physiological and physical constraints
↓
Latent hemodynamic parameters/state
Vascular resistance + arterial compliance + arterial load
↓
Short-term state/pressure trajectory
The underlying assumption is that the measured signals contain complementary information about cardiovascular function, but no single signal provides a complete representation of the underlying hemodynamic state.

Identifiability Framework
A major component of the project will be to investigate identifiability under progressively richer observation regimes.
Regime 1: PPG only
Can latent vascular parameters be recovered from peripheral pulse information alone?

Regime 2: PPG + ECG
Does information about cardiac electrical timing improve parameter recovery?

Regime 3: PPG + ECG + ABP
How much does continuous arterial pressure improve identifiability?

Regime 4: PPG + ECG + ABP + intervention information
Does knowledge of physiological perturbations, such as fluid or vasopressor administration, further constrain the latent state?

The comparison will quantify how parameter recovery changes as information is added.
Potential evaluation measures include:
Parameter estimation error
Relative error
Bias
Robustness to measurement noise
Sensitivity to missing signals
Uncertainty in parameter estimates
Identifiability under different observation regimes

Dataset Strategy
Public-Data Constraint
The ideal dataset would contain simultaneous high-frequency ECG, PPG and continuous arterial pressure, reliable intervention timestamps, long longitudinal trajectories, and a large cohort with septic shock.

No single fully public resource is expected to satisfy all of these requirements simultaneously.
The project therefore adopts a layered public-data strategy, in which each resource serves a specific methodological purpose rather than attempting to force one dataset to answer every research question.
MIMIC-BP
MIMIC-BP will serve as the primary public ICU waveform resource for signal characterisation and multimodal waveform analysis.
The dataset provides synchronised ECG, PPG and ABP segments, allowing investigation of:
Signal quality
Synchronisation
ECG–PPG timing
Pulse-wave morphology
Cardiovascular feature distributions
Multimodal representation learning
Short-segment physiological dynamics
Because the available segments are relatively short, MIMIC-BP will primarily support signal-level and representation-level analysis rather than long-term clinical trajectory modelling.

VitalDB
VitalDB will provide complementary high-frequency peri-operative waveform data and documented interventions.
It will be used to investigate:
High-frequency waveform morphology
ECG–PPG–ABP relationships
Pulse timing and morphology
Physiological responses around documented interventions
Short-term changes in arterial pressure and waveform characteristics
VitalDB is not treated as a septic-shock cohort. Its value in this project is its high-resolution multimodal waveform data and availability of intervention information.

Reduced-Order Cardiovascular Simulator
A reduced-order cardiovascular model will provide the controlled ground-truth environment.
A Windkessel-type or related multi-compartment model will be used to generate physiological signals from known parameters.
The simulator will allow controlled variation of:
Vascular resistance
Arterial compliance
Cardiac output-related parameters
Arterial load
Measurement noise
Physiological perturbations

Synthetic ECG-, PPG-, and ABP-like observations will be generated from known latent parameters.
Because the true parameters are known, the simulator provides the only environment in which true parameter-recovery error and identifiability can be directly quantified.

Beat-to-beat variability

Objective 3 — Develop a reduced-order cardiovascular simulator
Implement and validate a controllable cardiovascular model capable of generating multimodal physiological observations from known latent parameters.
Measurement noise and observation limitations will be incorporated to approximate real-world conditions.

Objective 4 — Quantify parameter identifiability
Systematically evaluate recovery of latent parameters under:
PPG alone
PPG + ECG
PPG + ECG + ABP
PPG + ECG + ABP + intervention information
The analysis will determine which latent parameters can be recovered reliably and under what observation conditions.

Objective 5 — Investigate short-horizon trajectory forecasting
Determine whether the estimated latent state contains useful information about subsequent short-term arterial pressure dynamics.
Forecasting will initially be evaluated on controlled synthetic trajectories and suitable clean public waveform segments.

Objective 6 — Establish limitations and translational requirements
Clearly define the limitations arising from:
Short public waveform segments
Peri-operative rather than septic-shock populations
Lack of continuous ground truth for latent parameters
Limited intervention information in some datasets
Differences between simulated and real physiological signals
The study will identify what additional data would be required for future validation in septic shock.

Feasibility
The narrowed project is designed to remain feasible under a strict public-data constraint.
MIMIC-BP and VitalDB provide complementary real-world waveform resources, while the cardiovascular simulator provides complete control over the latent parameters and experimental conditions. The use of simulation is particularly important because real clinical waveform datasets generally do not provide continuous ground-truth measurements of latent quantities such as systemic vascular resistance and arterial compliance. The project therefore does not depend on obtaining a large septic-shock cohort at the current stage.

Key Limitations
The following limitations will be explicitly acknowledged:
MIMIC-BP contains relatively short waveform segments, limiting longitudinal analysis.
VitalDB primarily represents peri-operative patients rather than septic-shock patients.
Public waveform datasets do not provide continuous ground truth for all latent hemodynamic parameters.
Intervention information is more limited in some datasets than others.
Synthetic cardiovascular models simplify the complexity and heterogeneity of real human physiology.
Successful parameter recovery in simulation does not establish clinical validity.
Translation to septic-shock care will require subsequent validation using appropriately characterised clinical datasets.

Expected Contribution
The primary contribution of the study will not be a new septic-shock classifier. Instead, it will be a methodological framework for investigating the observability and identifiability of latent hemodynamic states from multimodal physiological waveforms.
The study will establish:
What information is contained in public ECG, PPG and ABP recordings.
Which latent cardiovascular parameters can theoretically be recovered from these signals.
How additional physiological observations improve identifiability.
Whether physics-informed constraints improve recovery compared with purely data-driven approaches.
Whether estimated latent states provide useful information for short-term pressure trajectory forecasting.
What data and validation are required before the framework can be translated to septic-shock research.
Ultimately, the work aims to provide a principled bridge between physiological signal measurement, cardiovascular physics, inverse modelling, and computational estimation of hidden hemodynamic states.
